# How GlobaLeads22 Generates Valuable Leads

*A plain-language explainer for client conversations — and for your own reference.*

---

## The one-sentence version

> GlobaLeads22 finds businesses that have a **visible reason to buy what you sell**, then hands you the contact and a ready-made reason to reach out.

Most lead tools give you a giant list of names. That's the easy part — anyone can buy a list. The hard part is knowing *who actually needs you right now and why*. That's the gap GlobaLeads22 fills.

---

## The problem it solves

Imagine you sell websites. You could cold-call 500 businesses and hope a few need a new site. Most won't, and you'll waste days finding the few that do.

Now imagine instead you got a shortlist of businesses whose **current website is clearly outdated, has no booking button, and no contact form** — plus the owner's contact details and a one-line reason to open the conversation. You'd close far more, in far less time.

That shortlist is what GlobaLeads22 produces.

## How it works, in five plain steps

Think of the app as a **research assistant** that does in minutes what a smart intern would take days to do by hand.

**1. You tell it three things:**
- **What you sell** (e.g. web design, SEO, booking automation, paid ads).
- **Who you want to reach** (e.g. dentists, gyms, law firms, restaurants).
- **Where** (a city, region, or country).

**2. It finds the businesses.**
The app pulls a list of real, matching businesses in that area — their name, address, phone, category, website, and how well-reviewed they are.

**3. It looks at each business the way a prospect-hunter would.**
For every business that has a website, it reads the **public parts of their online presence** — their homepage, contact page, and similar — the same pages anyone could visit. It's looking for evidence, not secrets.

**4. It spots the "opportunity signals."**
This is the heart of the product. An **opportunity signal** is a visible sign that the business could use what you sell. For example:
- *No online booking* → great for someone selling booking automation.
- *Outdated or thin website* → great for a web designer.
- *No clear "contact us" or call-to-action* → great for lead-generation or web work.
- *Only a generic info@ email, no real person* → great for CRM or outreach setups.
- *Few reviews / weak local presence* → great for reputation or SEO services.

The app only looks for the signals **you said matter to your service**, so the results stay relevant to what you actually offer.

**5. It ranks and hands you the opportunities.**
Each business is scored on how strong its signals are and how relevant it is to your service. You get the **best-fit prospects first**, each with:
- the company details,
- a **likely decision-maker** contact where one can be found publicly,
- the **evidence** behind each signal (so you know *why* it's a fit),
- and the raw material for an **outreach angle** — a specific, honest reason to reach out.

## What makes these leads valuable

The difference is **evidence**. A normal lead is just "here's a dentist." A GlobaLeads22 lead is "here's a dentist whose website has no booking button and hasn't been updated in years — and here's the owner's contact." 

You're not guessing. You walk into every conversation already knowing the problem you can solve for them. That's what turns a cold list into warm, high-conversion outreach.

## Plain answers to questions that often come up

**"Where does the data come from? Is this allowed?"**
It uses **public information** — the same business listings and public web pages anyone can see online. There's no hidden or private data involved.

**"Is the contact guaranteed to be the decision-maker?"**
No tool can guarantee that. The app finds the **most likely** decision-maker from public sources and tells you how confident it is. Treat it as a strong starting point, not a promise.

**"Will it find a contact for every business?"**
Not always — some businesses simply don't publish one. Those still show up as opportunities; you just may need to reach them another way.

**"Does it write my sales message for me?"**
It gives you the **evidence and the angle** — the specific reason to reach out. You (or, increasingly, the app) turn that into the actual message.

**"What's the catch with low-result searches?"**
If a search returns few results, it usually means there wasn't much useful public evidence to work with — not that the area is empty. Broadening the location or signals usually helps.

## How the product charges (so you can explain pricing simply)

Everything runs on **credits** — one simple currency. A search spends credits based on how deep you want it to go (a quick scan costs less; a deeper search with decision-maker enrichment costs more). There are no confusing per-feature fees to explain; it's just "this search costs X credits."
